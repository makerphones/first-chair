# Reference — prior work, for the form pass

**These are the maker's own prior professional headphone designs**, from the HeadRoom Cosmic
programme (planar-magnetic over-ear, taken to production prototype and independently measured
by InnerFidelity) and the shape studies that preceded it. They are here so a design session can
be seeded from *what was actually built* instead of from adjectives describing it.

Captions matter as much as the pixels — read them.

the question the current pass is asking — and it shows the answer was never one outline.

## The headband blanks

`bow-blanks-cut-and-formed.jpg` — laser-cut blue-tempered spring steel. Flat blanks at left,
formed arcs at right.

Read the cut: a tapered strip that **splits into a Y-fork** toward the ear end, with the slots
**terminated in teardrops** so a cut cannot start a crack, and a mounting hole at each tip.
Material follows the bending moment — wide where the load is, split and slender where it is not.
This is "shape the silhouette, don't thin the slab," executed in steel years before it was
written down as a rule.

**What worked and what didn't:** the *cutting* is resolved. The *forming* failed — the arc came
out wrong and the spring rate too low, using printed forming jigs. That is why the current build
buys a bow. It is also why "make our own" is a sourcing conversation with a spring house rather
than a design problem: the part is already drawn.

## The intent, written at the time

From the design letter to the industrial designer on that product:

> "The code name Old Faithful is chosen to represent something that lasts a lifetime and will be
> passed down for generations. It should exude quality craftsmanship, **almost looking handmade
> but with extreme precision**. Parts should be easily replaceable and durable. Reduce weight
> where ever possible."

## The rules, taken from corrections given to that designer

1. **If a mechanism isn't needed, delete it.** A fold hinge was killed with *"an extra weakness
   we do not need."* Not "unnecessary" — a **weakness**.
2. **No ornament.** On a decorative step added where the pad met the cup: *"I am assuming this is
   for aesthetic reasons? I do not have to have this."*
3. **Simplify until it's simple, then stop before it's ugly.** *"There seems to be too much going
   on with the headband mechanism right now, we'd like to simplify"* — and *"we're looking to
   simplify without looking ugly. Hopefully we can find a nice middle ground."* Both halves.
4. **Material follows load.** On a yoke: *"if we don't need the extra metal we can save weight —
   but it does look nice and beefy."*
5. **The prototype settles arguments, not the drawing.** Hinge pins moved 8 mm because a built
   part said so.

## Provenance

Everything in this folder is the maker's own photography of his own prototypes, his own shape
sketches, and his own printed part.

**Deliberately excluded:** commissioned industrial-design renders and CAD from Deep Blue Design
and Martin Ortiz (ownership likely but unconfirmed), third-party product photography from the
